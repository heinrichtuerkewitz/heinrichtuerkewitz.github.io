Errors in the genealogical literature

Checking the statements handed down in the literature was always an essential driving force of my genealogical work. Even in large and recognised standard works, errors or statements that do not stand up to closer examination occasionally turn up.

This applies, among others, to the Adelslexikon of the Genealogisches Handbuch des Adels (Lexicon of the Nobility), published from 1972 to 2012, which undoubtedly counts among the most important recent reference works on noble genealogy. In the course of my research I repeatedly came across statements that had probably been taken over from older tradition but presented themselves differently on the basis of the sources.

As an example, the two different families of the knights Hermann von Hermannsdorff may be mentioned here, which were combined into a single overall lineage in the literature and also in the Adelslexikon. In the Silesian family Herrmann von Kattern, which I treated in the same study and which is not, however, listed in the Adelslexikon of the Genealogisches Handbuch des Adels, the statements of the literature also proved to be largely incomplete and erroneous.

It should be noted here that precisely that stratum, once widespread across Europe, of patricians, lesser nobility and significant bourgeois families, where any information and literary mentions can be found at all, is often particularly incomplete and poorly researched, although these too represented extremely interesting parts of our European history. In particular in the territory of the former Imperial and Royal (k. u. k.) Monarchy as well as in Hungary and Poland there still exists today a hardly manageable number of such families, which, probably owing to their sheer number alone, can never all be fully researched, but many of which, considered individually, already offer remarkable insights into the history of their region and their time.
